## Oct 29 (day 4)

- **Middle-ground drivetrain for Esper:** a NEMA 14 or pancake NEMA 17 on **DRV8834** drivers (2.5–10.8V) would run from the 5V battery. Not bought yet; the 28BYJ-48 stays as the fallback.
- **Servos without the PCA9685:** the ESP32's hardware PWM can drive them directly (GPIO 25, 13, 14), with servo power from the battery, not the 3V3 pin. Not changed yet.
- **Suction gripper:** the 12 mm cup arrived. **The peristaltic pump is too weak/slow** for it. New plan:
  - **Pump:** a 12V self-priming diaphragm pump (uxcell or CrocSee, 1.3 L/min), with the cup on the inlet.
  - **Release valve:** a diaphragm pump **can't reverse**, because its check valves set the direction. A normally-closed solenoid valve on a T vents the line to drop the coin. Desktop arms (uArm, Dobot) and vending pickers release the same way.
  - **Valve choice:** the **AIRZOE 2V025-08**, 12V, outlet wire. It's direct-acting, so it works on vacuum. The 4V210 is pilot-operated and won't.
  - **Sequence:** pump on to grip and hold; pump off and the valve on for ~0.3 s to drop.
  - **Switching:** a **Nano** over USB from the Pi, so a 12V mistake can't reach the Pi 5. Two **30N06L logic-level MOSFET** modules (D2 pump, D3 valve), with a 1N4007 across each load. No H-bridge.
- **Frame bring-up** (separate session):
  - The controller is an **Uno + CNC Shield running GRBL** (not RAMPS + Mega), on the Pi at `/dev/ttyACM0`.
  - The Pi is `ash@raspberrypi.local`.
  - X and Z moves confirmed. A reversed CoreXY motor would be fixed with `$3`.
  - The shared enable pin makes the idle motors click at the start and end of a move. That's normal.

## Oct 31 (day 6)

- **~17:40 The Uno started dropping off USB on every move** (`serial: device reports readiness to read but returned no data`). Tracked down:
  - Not the Pi's power: no under-voltage, and a proper supply instead of the Mac's USB-C didn't help.
  - **With the Nano unplugged, 5 move cycles ran with 0 drops.** Plugging it back in dropped the Uno at once.
  - **Cause: a ground loop.** The Nano's ground is tied to 12V − (the MOSFETs need it) and its USB goes to the Pi, so stepper return current found a second path through both USB cables and the Pi. That shifts the Uno's USB ground and resets its USB chip. All the Pi's USB ports share one ground, so changing ports doesn't help.
  - Two Pi "crashes" in between were the Pi 5's power button being bumped (`Power key pressed short` in the log).
- **Fix: GRBL drives the gripper, no Nano.** MOSFET gates moved to the CNC Shield: pump on **CoolEn** (`M8`/`M9`, checked at 5V with a meter), valve on **SpnDir** (`M4 S0` opens, `M3 S0` closes). SpnEn can't be used: in this variable-spindle build it's the Z limit input. No reflash. 5 move cycles with grip and drop each cycle: **0 errors, 0 USB drops.**
- **Hub on port 8000 now runs the gripper** (`/api/gripper/...`, Pump/Valve/Grip/Drop on the jog page). The port-8001 gripper service is disabled.
- **Overhead camera works on the Pi 5** (Camera v2, imx219, now on the Pi 5's own port). `GET /api/capture` returns a 1640×1232 JPEG in about 0.04 s, from a camera kept open by the hub. Still needs a mount ~55–60 cm above the table, centered, looking straight down.
- **Still open:**
  - Steps/mm (`$100`–`$102` = 50) hasn't been measured.
  - GRBL starts in alarm and needs `$H` or `$X`.
  - Camera mount and pixel → gantry calibration.
- **Esper moves to the Freenove ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM** (ESP32-S3 rev v0.2, 8 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM, CH343 USB at `/dev/cu.usbmodem5C930529801`, MAC `44:b1:76:c7:cd:74`). At first macOS didn't see it at all; a replug fixed it.
- **Dropped from Esper:** the VL6180X edge sensor and the PCA9685. Servos run from the S3's PWM on GPIO 38–40; the OLED is the only I2C device. Pins were then regrouped so each wheel is one 4-pin row on the right header: wheel 1 on 1, 2, 42, 41, wheel 2 on 45, 48, 47, 21, OLED on 20/19 (pin order from Freenove's ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 diagram). The RGB LED on 48 is lost as a mood light. Nothing on Esper stops it at the table edge now; that's the hub's job.
- **The bundled camera is a GC0308** (640×480), not an OV2640. It has no JPEG encoder, so the firmware encodes in software (~370 ms per frame).
- **The running camera wrecked the Wi-Fi:** pings went from ~5 ms to 170–300 ms and a 25 KB snapshot took 1–17 s. Camera off: normal. A 10 MHz clock helped only partly. Fix: the camera clock runs only during a capture or stream. Result: idle ping ~11 ms, snapshot ~0.9 s, MJPEG stream ~2 fps, `/status` still ~20–40 ms while streaming.
- **New firmware** `software/esper/firmware/`: API, wheels, servos, OLED, `GET /capture` (JPEG), `:81/stream` (MJPEG), `POST /camera` (flip). Wheels, servo and capture tested over Wi-Fi with nothing wired yet.
- **Orientation:** camera and OLED are mounted upside down. The camera now starts rotated 180°; the OLED uses rotation 0. The GC0308 driver silently ignores vflip (only hmirror works), so the firmware flips raw frames itself before encoding.
- **No pan or tilt servos;** the flipper is the only servo (GPIO 40). 38 and 39 are free.
- **The OLED shows bitmaps now:** `POST /screen` takes 1024-byte frames (128×64, 1 bit per pixel), up to 64 at once, looped at `X-FPS`. Without a picture it shows a status screen ("not connected" or the IP). The control page has a drawable screen preview and a text box that renders into it; a drawn frame reaches Esper in ~50 ms. The OLED was found on the S3 at 0x3C on the new pins.
- **Gotcha:** the ESP32 `WebServer` doesn't parse query args when it hands over a raw body, so the frame rate is a header (`X-FPS`), not `?fps=`.
- **Control page driving:** an arrow pad (and keyboard arrows) plus an accel box. On the bench, both wheels + turns Esper anticlockwise, so the wheels face opposite ways: ← = +/+, → = −/−, ↑ forward = s1 −, s2 + (checked on the bench). Speed and accel are sliders.
- **Battery resets:** on the power bank Esper dropped off Wi-Fi when the servo moved. The servo is an **MG996R** (not an MG90S): up to ~2.5 A stalled, against the bank's 5V/3A USB-A port. Capacitors (2× 470 µF) and a 1N5819 diode in the ESP32's 5V feed didn't help. A restart-reason readout showed **brownouts**, not the bank cutting out. Fix in firmware: brownout reset off (`esp_brownout_disable()`; the raw register write boot-loops the S3) and Wi-Fi transmit power at 8.5 dBm. After that it stayed up on the bank through servo and wheel moves. Still a risk: a servo held stalled against its end stop, or a low bank.
- **Faces:** `software/esper/faces/` holds ready-made screen animations, drawn in code by `make.py`: blinking, glancing **eyes** (56 frames, 12 fps) a pecking **chicken** (4 frames, 3 fps), and a **portrait**: a cartoon of the person on Esper's camera, smiling, blinking and waving (20 frames, 6 fps). A dithered photo didn't work: the person was backlit by a window and came out as a dark blob at 128×64, so a drawn cartoon reads far better. All played on Esper.
- **API tidy-up:** the full Esper API reference is in `software/esper/README.md` (every endpoint, field, error and a typical agent loop). `/info` keys are now `max_steps`, `max_speed`, `max_accel`. `/screen` takes `X-Repeat` (0 = forever, N = play N times then hold the last frame), and `/status` reports `screen.done`. Tested: play-once finished in ~1 s, play-3× in ~3 s.
- **Faces on the control page:** a grid of 19 animations drawn in the browser (eyes, chicken, happy, wink, love, sleepy, surprised, angry, dizzy, talking, heartbeat, bounce, rain, stars, code, spinner, fireworks, countdown, marquee), with a page preview and a loop/once switch. Tested on Esper: sent in ~130 ms, play-once ends with `done: true`.
- **Servo speed:** `/servo` takes an optional `speed` (1–600 °/s); Esper sweeps the flipper there in 10 ms steps, and `/status` shows `servo_now` and `servo_moving`. No speed = jump, as before. Tested: 90°→120° at 30°/s arrived in ~1 s. The page has a flipper speed slider.
- **Flipper resting pose:** 20°. Esper saves the flipper angle to flash once it has settled (2 s, wheels stopped). At boot it starts at the saved angle (no jump) and sweeps to 20° at 40°/s while Wi-Fi connects. Tested: moved to 60°, restarted, it came back at 60° and parked at 20°.
